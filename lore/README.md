## Invictus Empire — AI Agent Lore Guide

This guide is the entry point and operating contract for contributors writing or revising the Invictus Empire lore snippets. It exists to keep the series coherent across hundreds of entries: same world, same pressure, same distinction between rumor and truth, without making every snippet sound as though it came from the same mold.

## Agent Quick Start

Before writing or revising a snippet, do this in order:

1. Read this guide for tone, pacing, reveal rules, and canon hierarchy.
2. Read the [continuity ledger](continuity-ledger.md) for public belief, private knowledge, actual truth, open threads, and entity state.
3. Read the current-state snapshot in [the canon snippets](snippets.md).
4. Read the last 2-4 entries in [the canon snippets](snippets.md) to match immediate continuity and prose rhythm.
5. Choose one main movement for the snippet: a consequence, reveal, faction move, ritual step, public reaction, or private correction.
6. Write the snippet so it changes at least one pressure in the Empire.
7. Update the [continuity ledger](continuity-ledger.md) immediately after adding or replacing a snippet.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

- Canon hierarchy: existing snippet text → continuity ledger → lore guide → new invention.
- Keep public belief, private knowledge, actual truth, official record, and erased record separate.
- Do not fully explain the Throne, the Sovereign, or the deepest Archive truths too early.
- A faction must move for a reason: grain, law, roads, armies, faith, marriage, memory, legitimacy, fear, or survival.
- Every snippet must advance the series. Atmosphere alone is not enough.
- Treat metadata as navigation. If metadata conflicts with canon prose, canon prose wins and the ledger must be corrected.

## 1. Core Tone

Write in a mythic, imperial, serious tone.

The prose should feel:

- ceremonial, but not decorative;
- severe, but not melodramatic;
- ominous, but not vague;
- ancient, but not archaic to the point of stiffness;
- political, legal, and human beneath the grandeur.

Avoid modern phrasing, jokes, self-aware lines, and cheap "epic" overload. The best Invictus line sounds inevitable after it has been read, not loud before it has earned weight.

## 2. What Each Snippet Must Do

Each snippet must do at least one of these:

- reveal a new truth about the Empire;
- deepen the mystery of the Throne or Sovereign;
- introduce an important faction, person, ritual, city, war, law, relic, betrayal, or prophecy;
- show consequences spreading through the Empire;
- reframe something previously believed to be true;
- move a faction, institution, province, or household into a new posture.

A snippet should advance, not merely decorate. If removing it would change nothing, it is probably filler.

## 3. Canon Hierarchy

When writing new snippets, treat canon in this order of authority:

1. Established snippet prose already written.
2. The continuity ledger.
3. This lore guide.
4. New invention for the current snippet.

If a new idea conflicts with an earlier snippet, the earlier snippet wins unless the contradiction is intentional and framed as propaganda, false history, rumor, censorship, error, or later revelation.

Never casually overwrite canon.

## 4. Continuity Layers

Future agents must track and preserve these layers at all times:

- what is publicly believed;
- what is privately known;
- what is actually true;
- who knows which version;
- what the Empire has officially recorded;
- what has been erased, censored, withheld, or falsified.

Confusion inside the world is useful. Confusion in the writing is not.

When a fact changes layers, make that change visible. For example, a palace rumor may become private knowledge, or an Archive record may correct an official lie without becoming public.

## 5. Reveal Budget

Do not let every snippet contain a major revelation.

Use this reveal ladder:

- Minor reveal: new detail, omen, law, title, symbol, relationship, procedure, route, or local custom.
- Medium reveal: hidden motive, old crime, new claimant possibility, faction shift, buried record, or private proof.
- Major reveal: succession truth, dynastic lie, sacred law, civil fracture, war trigger, public identity confirmation, or irreversible legal recognition.

Recommended rhythm:

- 1 major reveal every 8-15 snippets.
- 1 medium reveal every 3-5 snippets.
- Minor reveals may appear often, but must matter.

Do not stack multiple major reveals in consecutive snippets unless entering a turning-point arc.

## 6. Snippet Format

Each snippet should usually contain:

- title;

- one main movement;
- atmospheric opening;
- development through law, memory, fear, logistics, ritual, or consequence;
- a reveal, complication, or pressure shift;

## 7. Series Pillars

These foundations should recur across the saga:

- The Throne is ancient, symbolic, judging, and almost alive in meaning.
- The Sovereign is central, but should not be fully explained too early.
- The Empire must feel huge: noble houses, provinces, armies, temples, roads, laws, traitors, myths, and supply chains.
- Power versus worth is a major theme.
- Endurance matters more than beauty, noise, popularity, or ceremony.
- Memory matters: history, forgotten rulers, buried truth, false legacy, and records that outlive winners.
- Inevitability should build slowly. Events may feel fated, but never lazy.

## 8. Arc Discipline

For a 400-1000 snippet run, reveal slowly.

Use this broad pacing:

- Snippets 1-20: mystery, symbols, first reactions, legal mechanisms, visible consequences.
- Snippets 20-50: important factions, key names, rival powers, old crimes, first overt movements.
- Snippets 50-100: confrontations, declarations, faction breaks, public pressure, provincial fracture.
- Snippets 100-200: deep history, betrayals, foreign powers, succession truths, sacred laws.
- Snippets 200-400+: wars, purges, ascension, collapse, reform, legacy, myth becoming history.

General pressure rhythm:

- Every 5-10 snippets: one notable reveal or strong consequence.
- Every 20-30 snippets: a real shift in the balance of power.
- Every 50+ snippets: a major turning point.

## 9. Faction Logic

Every faction move must have a reason.

When a house, temple, legion, archive, province, or claimant acts, future agents should know:

- what it wants;
- what it fears;
- what it can actually do;
- what it cannot afford to lose;
- what old grievance or duty shapes its conduct;
- what public language it uses to disguise private intent.

No faction should exist only to look impressive.

Power in the Empire moves through inheritance, grain, roads, armies, faith, law, marriage, memory, and legitimacy. Ambition alone is too vague.

## 10. Naming And Title Discipline

Do not introduce too many capitalized names too fast.

Rules:

- One major new proper noun per snippet is usually enough.
- If multiple names are introduced, one must be the clear focus.
- Names should imply rank, region, tradition, function, or institutional memory.
- Avoid fantasy-noise names with no cultural logic.
- Titles should stay consistent once established.
- Use capitalized ritual/legal terms consistently: First Lamp through Ninth Lamp, Restraint, Measure, Archive, Throne, Regent, Sovereign, Nine Houses.

Track every named thing by:

- first appearance;
- allegiance;
- status;
- current relevance;
- whether common people know it, or only elites do.

## 11. Scale And Time Pressure

The story must not feel as though events float in sacred fog.

At regular intervals, establish active time pressure through:

- a deadline in law;
- a ritual date;
- a funeral period;
- a military march;
- planting, harvest, winter, or hunger;
- a provincial response delay;
- a decree spreading by road, river, beacon, or rumor;
- a countdown imposed by custom.

At least every few snippets, remind the reader that the Empire is enormous. Show scale through provinces reacting at different speeds, roads, ports, forts, legal lag, cultural differences, and the distance between palace truth and frontier truth.

## 12. Myth, Record, And Bias

The setting should constantly play with the gap between:

- what was sung;
- what was written;
- what was ordered into record;
- what was erased;
- what truly happened.

Future agents may use imperial chronicles, temple recitations, soldier testimony, household rumor, censored archive fragments, funeral inscriptions, market prices, wayhouse ledgers, and foreign accounts as truth-containers.

No source is neutral by default. Every source should have a bias, limitation, fear, or purpose.

Label major lore internally as one of these:

- imperial record;
- censored archive;
- court rumor;
- house tradition;
- temple doctrine;
- soldier testimony;
- provincial memory;
- foreign account;
- confirmed event;
- uncertain claim.

## 13. Category Rotation

Rotate snippet types to prevent the Empire from shrinking into one hall.

Useful categories:

- Throne: presence, history, judgment, symbolism.
- Sovereign: rumors, actions, signs, enemies, doctrine, absence.
- Empire: cities, provinces, roads, armies, laws, succession, administration.
- House: noble families, rivalries, bloodlines, grudges, marriages, treason.
- Military: legions, generals, campaigns, banners, defeats, doctrines.
- Faith/ritual: temples, funeral rites, coronation rites, forbidden prayers, relics.
- History: old emperors, failed claimants, lost wars, erased dynasties.
- Commoner: markets, bread, taverns, servants, soldiers, rumor, fear.
- Enemy: rebels, foreign kingdoms, traitors, pretenders, cults, exiles.
- Consequence: what changed because of the last few snippets.
- Archive/legal: records, corrections, sealed law, censored memory, old offices.

## 14. Ending Variety

Strong closing lines matter, but they should not all strike the same note.

Rotate ending types:

- decree;
- threat;
- omen;
- historical reframe;
- irreversible consequence;
- moral judgment;
- vow;
- rumor made dangerous;
- silence loaded with meaning;
- legal correction;
- logistical consequence.

The end of a snippet should always push forward, but not always with the same rhythm.

## 15. Prose Guardrails

The voice should stay unified, but the cadence should not become predictable.

Watch for overuse of:

- "There are..."
- "For..."
- "And though..."
- "Not this. Not that."
- stacked single-line negations;
- repeated final lines that end with the same kind of blow;
- overusing "older than" as the only way to create age;
- every faction acting through perfect silence instead of varied forms of pressure.

Recent cadence notes from snippets I-XV:

- The series has used silence, waiting, and counting effectively. Future snippets should begin varying the opening motion: movement of carts, a legal reading, a prayer interrupted, a border report, a household correction, a public price, a road delay.
- The "not rebellion, but loyalty" tension is strong around Veyrwatch. Do not repeat the same paradox too many times before changing the stakes.
- Hunger and law are now active public pressures. Use ordinary details when returning to the capital.
- The princess should remain observant and restrained. Do not make her suddenly speak like a ruler before the story earns it.

## 16. Anchor Canon

These truths are load-bearing and must not be casually altered:

- The Throne recognized someone unseen.
- The Hall fell silent without proclamation.
- The Nine Houses reacted and began maneuvering.
- The Archive of Ash preserves censored truths and can correct official memory.
- The Regent is a steward under law, not master of the Throne.
- The Restraint of the Ninth Day is active.
- The Nine Lamps are the public countdown to Measure.
- The Golden Sickle has slowed grain while appearing loyal.
- The Iron Laurel has begun readiness while appearing lawful.
- The princess exists as Daughter of the Unproclaimed and is under Custody of Continuance.
- Veyrwatch deliberately withheld answer under the logic of the Unended Watch.
- The Sovereign's full identity, origin, and motive remain unrevealed.

Any future snippet touching an anchor must deepen it, complicate it, or show its consequences. It must not ignore it.

## 17. Ledger Format

After each snippet, log the following in the same order every time:

- Snippet number.
- Title.
- Main subject.
- Public belief affected.
- Private knowledge affected.
- Actual truth affected.
- New lore introduced.
- Named characters/factions/places added.
- Open questions created.
- Balance-of-power change.
- Timeline position.
- Scene-position tags.
- Tone note.
- Future hooks.

This keeps canon usable, not merely recorded.

## 18. Entity State Tracking

For every recurring character, house, temple, legion, province, or institution, maintain a separate state entry.

Track:

- current goal;
- current fear;
- public posture;
- private intent;
- present strength;
- present weakness;
- key dependency;
- unresolved grievance;
- what changed in its last appearance.

Do not write a faction as though it is frozen between snippets.

## 19. Macro-Arc Skeleton

Use this broad skeleton:

- Arc I — The Waiting Ends: the Throne reacts, rumors spread, the court senses change.
- Arc II — The Houses Stir: noble families maneuver, alliances and fear begin forming.
- Arc III — The Name in Shadow: the future sovereign becomes a real force, but still not fully seen.
- Arc IV — The Empire Divides: power centers clash and old loyalties crack.
- Arc V — The First Claim: a formal challenge, decree, or act forces open conflict.
- Arc VI — Blood and Memory: the true history of the Throne and Empire starts surfacing.
- Arc VII — The Cost of Rule: victory, failure, sacrifice, judgment, reform, ruin.
- Arc VIII — What Endures: the final question becomes who was worthy, and what remains after them.
