## Detailed Snippet Ledger

<!-- LEDGER:I -->
### Snippet I — The Throne of Invictus

- **Main subject:** The ancient Throne as the central symbol, judge, and witness of the Empire.
- **Public belief affected:** The people of the Empire understand the Throne as the highest seat of rule, older and more enduring than crowns, kings, and claimants.
- **Private knowledge affected:** The court and old powers likely know the Throne is not merely symbolic, though its full nature remains unspoken.
- **Actual truth affected:** The Throne has been waiting, and that waiting has now ended.
- **New lore introduced:** The Throne is ancient, severe, enduring, and tied to worth rather than possession. It remembers rulers and false sovereigns.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** The Throne of Invictus; the Invictus Empire.
- **Open questions created:** What does it mean for the Throne to wait? Who or what has it been waiting for? How does the Throne judge worth? Is its memory literal, symbolic, or something older than both?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The center of legitimacy shifts away from ordinary rule, bloodline, and force toward the Throne's recognition.
- **Timeline position:** Opening state of the saga; immediately before or at the first sign of change.
- **Scene-position tags:** Court, Dynastic, Prophetic.
- **Tone note:** Foundational, ceremonial, ominous; establishes endurance over beauty and worth over possession.
- **Future hooks:** The awaited sovereign; the Throne's judgment; false rulers erased by time; the difference between holding power and being worthy of it.

### Snippet II — When the Hall Fell Silent

- **Main subject:** The moment the Throne recognizes someone unseen.
- **Public belief affected:** No formal public proclamation occurs yet, but witnesses understand that something historic has happened in the Hall.
- **Private knowledge affected:** Those present in the Hall know the Throne no longer feels empty and that the old order has changed without decree.
- **Actual truth affected:** The Throne has recognized someone, though no name, crown, or claimant has been revealed.
- **New lore introduced:** The Hall of the Throne reacts physically and spiritually to recognition: flames bend, banners fall still, and silence rises from the stone.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** The Hall of the Throne; great houses indirectly through their banners.
- **Open questions created:** Who has been recognized? Why now? What law or force allows the Throne to recognize someone before public succession begins? Who witnessed enough to understand the meaning?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The court's ability to pretend stability remains intact is broken. Legitimacy has moved ahead of politics.
- **Timeline position:** The first active event of Arc I, shortly after the Throne's waiting ends.
- **Scene-position tags:** Court, Ritual, Prophetic.
- **Tone note:** Quiet, heavy, inevitable; history arrives without ceremony.
- **Future hooks:** Witness accounts; court reaction; banners of the great houses; the unnamed recognized figure; the end of false normality.

### Snippet III — Beneath the Nine Banners

- **Main subject:** The Nine Houses begin to react to the Throne's recognition.
- **Public belief affected:** Publicly, the Nine Houses remain silent and controlled; no open challenge or declaration has been made.
- **Private knowledge affected:** The noble houses understand that succession has become dangerous because it is now tied to worth rather than influence, force, or arrangement.
- **Actual truth affected:** The Nine Houses are maneuvering behind closed doors, and their pride has been symbolically bent by the Throne's sign.
- **New lore introduced:** The Empire has Nine great houses whose banners hang above the Hall. These houses are ancient, powerful, and accustomed to shaping succession through blood, marriage, grudges, and regional power.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** The Nine Houses; the Nine Banners; river provinces; mountain fortresses; southern granaries; northern marches.
- **Open questions created:** Which houses are strongest? Which house fears the recognized sovereign most? Did all nine banners bow equally? Are the houses preparing alliance, resistance, submission, or betrayal?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The houses begin moving as political actors. The Throne's recognition forces them from passive pride into active calculation.
- **Timeline position:** Night following the silence in the Hall.
- **Scene-position tags:** Court, Dynastic, House, Provincial.
- **Tone note:** Political, restrained, tense; noble fear hidden behind ceremony.
- **Future hooks:** House rivalries; marriage pacts; old oaths; regional power bases; northern marches where imperial law is weaker.

### Snippet IV — The Archive of Ash

- **Main subject:** The hidden imperial archive awakens after the Hall's silence.
- **Public belief affected:** The public does not know the Archive has acted; palace whispers begin, but nothing is officially announced.
- **Private knowledge affected:** The wardens of the Archive know that the Throne's remembrance has triggered sealed records connected to succession, censored lineages, and erased claimants.
- **Actual truth affected:** The Empire's official history is incomplete or falsified. Certain lineages may not be extinct, and a denied claim may still exist.
- **New lore introduced:** The Archive of Ash preserves censored genealogies, erased decrees, removed claimants, dangerous judgments, and truths too necessary to destroy. The Chamber of Continuance and a sealed cedar chest are opened after a bell rings for the first time in 173 years.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** The Archive of Ash; the Chamber of Continuance; the wardens of the Archive.
- **Open questions created:** What was inside the cedar chest? Which lineage survived? Which victories were false? Who ordered past records altered? What claim has begun to breathe again?
- **Balance-of-power change:** Memory becomes political evidence. Bloodlines trusted as inheritance are now vulnerable to hidden records.
- **Timeline position:** Same night as the Hall's silence, continuing into sunrise.
- **Scene-position tags:** Archive, Dynastic, Legal, Aftermath.
- **Tone note:** Secretive, severe, memory-centered; history becomes a weapon.
- **Future hooks:** Censored genealogies; denied claim; old crimes; Archive intervention; conflict between public record and actual truth.

### Snippet V — The Regent Below the Steps

- **Main subject:** The Regent is forced back into his lawful role as steward, not ruler.
- **Public belief affected:** The Empire still sees the Regent as the acting center of government, but signs of control begin to shift through closed halls and sealed orders.
- **Private knowledge affected:** The Regent understands that the Throne, the Hall, and the Archive together have placed him under the Restraint of the Ninth Day.
- **Actual truth affected:** The Regent is bound by ancient interregnal law to summon the Nine Houses, unseal disputed inheritance lines, and surrender succession to older judgment within nine days.
- **New lore introduced:** The Regent's seat stands below the Throne's steps by design. The current Regent has governed for nineteen years. The Restraint of the Ninth Day exists because a former regent tried to found his own line and caused seven years of civil war.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** The Regent; the Restraint of the Ninth Day; the old legal chambers; captains of the inner guard.
- **Open questions created:** Does the Regent intend obedience, delay, or sabotage? Which ministers and guards are loyal to him? What happened in the earlier succession war? How much power has the Regent built during nineteen years?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The Regent's authority is narrowed. His seal still moves the government, but the command behind it belongs to older law.
- **Timeline position:** Morning after the Archive bell; first day of the nine-day restraint period.
- **Scene-position tags:** Court, Legal, Dynastic, Aftermath.
- **Tone note:** Judicial, restrained, humbling; borrowed power is exposed as conditional.
- **Future hooks:** Nine-day deadline; legal chambers opening; Regent's possible resistance; summons to the Nine Houses; old succession law returning.

### Snippet VI — The First Lamp

- **Main subject:** The Restraint becomes visible to the entire Empire through the lighting of the first of the Nine Lamps.
- **Public belief affected:** The public now knows succession is no longer rumor. The first lamp gives the crisis a visible countdown.
- **Private knowledge affected:** Nobles, priests, governors, captains, and old families understand the deeper danger of promises, alliances, and claims made under the Nine Lamps.
- **Actual truth affected:** Ancient law has moved from sealed chambers into public ritual. The Empire is now counting toward unavoidable judgment.
- **New lore introduced:** The Temple of Measure holds nine iron braziers used during the Restraint of the Ninth Day. Each evening one lamp is lit until the ninth flame forces hidden claims and bloodlines into judgment. Beacon towers carry the signal across roads, rivers, forts, bridges, vineyards, and fields.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** The Temple of Measure; the Nine Lamps; beacon towers.
- **Open questions created:** What happens when the ninth lamp burns out? Which claimants will be summoned? Which houses will act before judgment? What promises will be made under counting fire?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The succession crisis becomes public and imperial in scale. The Regent and the Nine Houses lose the ability to keep the matter confined to palace halls.
- **Timeline position:** Dusk of the first day of the Restraint.
- **Scene-position tags:** Ritual, Legal, Commoner, Provincial.
- **Tone note:** Ceremonial, expansive, controlled; the Empire's scale is shown through the spread of fire.
- **Future hooks:** Public fear; provincial reactions; promises made during the Nine Lamps; countdown to judgment; beacon network as imperial nervous system.

### Snippet VII — The Beacon That Did Not Burn

- **Main subject:** A northern fortress fails to answer the beacon chain, creating the first visible sign of provincial defiance or weakness.
- **Public belief affected:** Most people believe the Empire is still counting as one under the Nine Lamps.
- **Private knowledge affected:** The Regent, clerks, Archive, and Nine Houses know that Veyrwatch did not answer the First Lamp.
- **Actual truth affected:** The succession countdown has exposed a fracture in the northern marches, though the cause remains unconfirmed.
- **New lore introduced:** Imperial beacon chains require obedience from distant forts. Veyrwatch is a crucial northern fortress tied to coronation security, war confirmation, and control of the frost road.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** Veyrwatch; Harrow Ford; West Road; South Road; River Chain; Lowland Chain; Eastern Chain; the northern frost road.
- **Open questions created:** Did Veyrwatch fail by accident, neglect, or choice? Who controls it? Why does the Archive send a hidden rider? Is this the first act of rebellion? Will other forts follow the silence?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The Regent appears weaker, the northern command becomes suspect, and the Nine Houses learn that refusal may be possible outside the capital.
- **Timeline position:** Morning through sunset of the second day, after the First Lamp and during preparation of the Second Lamp.
- **Scene-position tags:** Provincial, Military, Legal, Aftermath.
- **Tone note:** Ominous escalation; imperial unity is challenged through absence rather than open battle.
- **Future hooks:** Northern marches; hidden Archive mission; Veyrwatch investigation; possible military fracture; defiance spreading before the ninth lamp.

### Snippet VIII — The Rider Without Heraldry

- **Main subject:** The Archive's hidden rider investigates the road to Veyrwatch and discovers that the fortress had enough beacon oil to answer the First Lamp.
- **Public belief affected:** The public still does not know Veyrwatch failed or that an Archive rider is investigating the frost road.
- **Private knowledge affected:** The Archive begins to suspect that Veyrwatch's silence was not simple accident or shortage. The northern record contains signs of old authority still moving beneath dead seals.
- **Actual truth affected:** Veyrwatch did not go dark because it lacked the means to burn. Its silence has a documented history tied to a seal from a dissolved northern office.
- **New lore introduced:** Archive riders investigate by comparing ledgers, witness statements, oil stores, and vanished records. A dead northern command seal has appeared on a recent supply receipt. Archive servants use hidden black wax marks to signal that records have become suspect.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** The hidden Archive rider; seventh tower north of Harrow Ford; dissolved northern frontier offices; dead northern command seal.
- **Open questions created:** Who used the old seal? Why was Veyrwatch supplied under dead authority? Who brought the receipt? Is the old northern administration truly dead, or has it survived beneath official records? Why did the rider avoid reporting directly to the Regent?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The Archive gains independent evidence that the northern fracture may be organized. The Regent loses sole control over the interpretation of Veyrwatch's silence.
- **Timeline position:** Second night into morning after the Second Lamp, during the northern investigation before the Third Lamp.
- **Scene-position tags:** Archive, Provincial, Military, Legal.
- **Tone note:** Investigative, cold, restrained; the mystery deepens through records rather than open confrontation.
- **Future hooks:** The old seal; hidden northern authority; Archive marks; Veyrwatch's supply chain; possible survival of dissolved offices; distrust between Archive and Regent.

### Snippet IX — The House of the Golden Sickle

- **Main subject:** One of the Nine Houses uses control of southern grain to create leverage during the Restraint while appearing loyal.
- **Public belief affected:** Common people notice bread prices rising and supply slowing, but they do not know this is deliberate house maneuvering.
- **Private knowledge affected:** The Nine Houses and their stewards understand that the House of the Golden Sickle is obeying in form while using grain as pressure.
- **Actual truth affected:** The House of the Golden Sickle has not chosen a claimant but has begun rationing obedience through delays in grain movement.
- **New lore introduced:** The House of the Golden Sickle is one of the Nine Houses and controls major southern granaries, river barges, flour routes, and food supply to the capital. In Invictus, grain is a political weapon equal to steel when cities and armies depend on it.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** House of the Golden Sickle; southern estate; southern granaries; river barges; flour halls; river market.
- **Open questions created:** Which claimant will the House of the Golden Sickle eventually support? Is it acting alone or coordinating with another house? How severe will the bread pressure become? Will the Regent confront them, bargain with them, or pretend not to see?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The succession crisis expands from ritual and law into material pressure. The Nine Houses learn that loyalty can be weaponized without open rebellion.
- **Timeline position:** Third day of the Restraint, as the Third Lamp is prepared and lit.
- **Scene-position tags:** House, Provincial, Commoner, Legal.
- **Tone note:** Political, economic, quietly threatening; obedience becomes another form of coercion.
- **Future hooks:** Food prices in the capital; unrest among commoners and soldiers; southern grain leverage; House of the Golden Sickle's future claimant choice; Regent's response to lawful obstruction.

### Snippet X — The Price of Bread

- **Main subject:** The capital begins to feel the House of the Golden Sickle's grain pressure through bread prices, thinner rations, and market fear.
- **Public belief affected:** Common people believe merchants, weather, taxes, and ordinary greed are responsible for rising bread prices; most do not know the delays are politically engineered.
- **Private knowledge affected:** The Regent, market officials, barracks captains, and noble stewards understand that the grain slowdown is tied to the Golden Sickle's lawful obstruction.
- **Actual truth affected:** Hunger has begun turning the succession crisis into a daily public reality, and obedience among commoners and soldiers is becoming thinner.
- **New lore introduced:** The capital uses Bread Tables overseen by Market Wardens to display lawful loaf prices and measures. Bread is treated as the daily covenant between imperial power and public obedience.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** Market Wardens; Bread Tables; lower barracks; western square.
- **Open questions created:** How much hunger can the capital absorb before unrest begins? Will the Regent fix prices, punish merchants, confront the Golden Sickle, or continue hiding behind records? How will soldiers react if rations continue shrinking?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The common people and barracks become active pressure points in the succession crisis. The Regent's inability to command food exposes the limits of seals and decrees.
- **Timeline position:** Morning after the Third Lamp through the lighting of the Fourth Lamp.
- **Scene-position tags:** Commoner, Court, Legal, Aftermath.
- **Tone note:** Social, tense, grounded; imperial crisis becomes visible through ordinary hunger.
- **Future hooks:** Bread unrest; soldiers' loyalty under ration pressure; Market Warden reports; Regent's possible crackdown; Golden Sickle's leverage becoming harder to conceal.

### Snippet XI — The House of the Iron Laurel

- **Main subject:** Another of the Nine Houses responds to the crisis by preparing military readiness under the appearance of lawful stability.
- **Public belief affected:** The public sees no open military break; any movement by the Iron Laurel can be framed as ceremonial escort or road protection.
- **Private knowledge affected:** The Nine Houses understand that the Iron Laurel is preparing soldiers, watching roads and granaries, and positioning itself against the consequences of hunger.
- **Actual truth affected:** A second great house has begun weaponizing obedience, not through grain but through readiness and disciplined force.
- **New lore introduced:** The House of the Iron Laurel is one of the Nine Houses, rooted in imperial military service, veteran captains, fortified estates, and road security. Its emblem is a black laurel bound around a spearhead.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** House of the Iron Laurel; black laurel and spearhead emblem; western road forts; minor forts loyal by old service.
- **Open questions created:** Will the Iron Laurel defend the Regent, the Restraint, its own interests, or the eventual Sovereign? Will its road-guarding bring it into conflict with the Golden Sickle? Which forts and captains still owe it loyalty?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The crisis now has a military counterweight to the Golden Sickle's economic pressure. The Nine Houses begin counting forces, not merely lamps.
- **Timeline position:** Fourth day of the Restraint, after bread reports and Veyrwatch rumors reach the Iron Laurel.
- **Scene-position tags:** House, Military, Legal, Provincial.
- **Tone note:** Disciplined, strategic, restrained; steel begins moving without declaring itself.
- **Future hooks:** House rivalry between grain and military power; road escorts; barracks loyalty; possible armed confrontation disguised as lawkeeping; the Iron Laurel's eventual side.

### Snippet XII — The Unended Watch

- **Main subject:** The hidden Archive rider finds stronger evidence that Veyrwatch's silence was deliberate and tied to an older northern command tradition.
- **Public belief affected:** The public still knows nothing about the Veyrwatch investigation or the Unended Watch.
- **Private knowledge affected:** The Archive now has evidence that Veyrwatch saw the First Lamp and chose not to answer. The Regent has not yet been informed directly.
- **Actual truth affected:** Veyrwatch's silence was not caused by ignorance, distance, oil shortage, or simple accident. It appears connected to a phrase or body called the Unended Watch, rooted in old northern authority.
- **New lore introduced:** Wayhouse ledgers on the frost road record passage and supply movement. Some frontier clerks used older book styles before centralization. The phrase Unended Watch appears in connection with Veyrwatch's deliberate silence.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** The Unended Watch; last wayhouse before the Veyrwatch ascent; charcoal burner messenger families.
- **Open questions created:** What is the Unended Watch? Is it an organization, oath, title, old garrison tradition, or hidden command? Does it serve a claimant, old law, northern grievance, or something else? Why has the Archive not told the Regent directly?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The Archive gains evidence that the northern fracture is organized and historically rooted. The Regent's understanding of the crisis falls further behind the Archive's.
- **Timeline position:** Fourth day into dusk, after the Fourth Lamp and before the deeper approach to Veyrwatch.
- **Scene-position tags:** Archive, Provincial, Military, Legal.
- **Tone note:** Cold, investigative, ominous; the northern silence becomes deliberate without fully revealing its master.
- **Future hooks:** Archive search of censored northern records; the Unended Watch; Veyrwatch ascent; dead northern command seal; hidden rider's message bypassing the Regent.

### Snippet XIII — The Princess Without Procession

- **Main subject:** A hidden royal child is introduced inside the palace as a withheld princess tied to the unproclaimed sovereign line.
- **Public belief affected:** The public still knows nothing of the child. Palace servants only know rumors of a controlled ward in the eastern wing, and even those rumors are whispered carefully.
- **Private knowledge affected:** The Archive now knows through the Registry of Living Continuance that the child is recorded as the Daughter of the Unproclaimed. The Regent's household has been managing her under false language.
- **Actual truth affected:** A living princess exists inside the palace, but her title, paternal designation, and dynastic position have been deliberately withheld until Measure. She is not yet a claimant, but she is a living consequence of the hidden line.
- **New lore introduced:** The eastern palace wing and winter garden house a protected royal child under the false title of ward. The Registry of Living Continuance records concealed births, disputed children, hostage-wards, removed heirs, and dangerous living bloodlines. The title Daughter of the Unproclaimed is introduced.
- **Named characters/factions/places added:** The hidden princess; the Little Lamp as a servant-whispered name; the eastern palace wing; the old winter garden; the Registry of Living Continuance; Daughter of the Unproclaimed.
- **Open questions created:** Who is the Unproclaimed? Who is the child's father? Why is her maternal record disputed? Did the Regent hide her for protection, control, or leverage? Who else knows she exists? Will the Nine Houses learn of her before the ninth lamp?
- **Balance-of-power change:** The succession crisis gains a living royal child inside the palace, giving the Archive leverage and making the Regent's controlled household records politically dangerous.
- **Timeline position:** Fifth morning through dusk of the Restraint, after the Archive receives the hidden rider's message and before the Fifth Lamp burns fully through the night.
- **Scene-position tags:** Court, Archive, Dynastic, Commoner.
- **Tone note:** Controlled, intimate, ominous; the crisis enters a private chamber without losing imperial weight.
- **Future hooks:** The princess's identity and protection; servant rumors spreading through the palace; the Registry of Living Continuance; the Regent's reason for withholding her title; whether the Throne's recognition affects her directly; possible attempts by houses, Archive, or Regent to use or protect her.

### Snippet XIV — The Cage Made Law

- **Main subject:** The Archive places the hidden princess under legal protection, limiting the Regent's private control over her.
